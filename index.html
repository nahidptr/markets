    How it works

    • Prices are month-end, dividend-adjusted (total return) prices of US-listed ETFs an ordinary investor could buy, so dividends are reinvested and fund fees are already taken out. Bitcoin is the spot price. Data runs from to .
    • Each purchase happens on a random day: its price is interpolated between the previous and current month-end. "Every month" buys mid-month. "Lump sum" invests everything on the first day of the start month.
    • Inflation: "After inflation" is the money-weighted return once each purchase is restated in end-date money. The dashed red line is what your cash would have needed to grow to just to keep its buying power. US dollars use the US CPI; other currencies use the yearly rate in Settings.
    • Bitcoin before 2013 traded on tiny, unreliable exchanges. Prices from 2010 are real but almost nobody could have bought meaningful amounts, so early-start Bitcoin results are an upper bound, not a realistic outcome.
    • Currencies: prices are converted from US dollars at each month's exchange rate, so a non-US investor sees currency moves too. Exchange rates are monthly averages from the US Federal Reserve.
    • Index proxies: FTSE uses EWU (MSCI UK), Swiss uses EWL (MSCI Switzerland), Sweden uses EWD (MSCI Sweden), Nifty 50 uses INDY, US small caps use IWM (Russell 2000). They track their markets closely but are not the exact local index.
    • Not included: personal taxes, brokerage fees, spreads and the cost of holding cash.
    • Past returns say little about the future. This is a history lesson, not financial advice.
