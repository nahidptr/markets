    How it works

    • Prices are month-end, dividend-adjusted (total return) prices of US-listed ETFs an ordinary investor could buy, so dividends are reinvested and fund fees are already taken out. Bitcoin is the spot price.
    • Same money everywhere. Each purchase goes into every asset on the same day and for the same amount. Random purchases land on a random day; the price is interpolated between month-ends. Monthly buys happen mid-month; a lump sum buys at the start of the first month.
    • Inflation. "After inflation" restates every purchase in end-date money and recomputes the annual return. "Keeping up with inflation" is what your cash needed to grow to just to keep its buying power.
    • Your currency. US-dollar prices are converted at each month's exchange rate, so a non-US investor also sees currency moves.
    • Index proxies. Country ETFs track their markets closely but are not the exact local index. Hover or long-press an asset to see which fund is used.
    • Bitcoin before 2013 traded on tiny exchanges. The prices are real, but almost nobody could have bought meaningful amounts, so treat early Bitcoin results as an upper bound.
    • Not included: personal taxes, brokerage fees and spreads.
    • Past returns say little about the future. This is a history lesson, not financial advice.
